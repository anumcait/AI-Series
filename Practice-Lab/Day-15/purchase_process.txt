ERP PURCHASE PROCESS

Purpose

The ERP purchase process defines how employees request and purchase
goods or services for the organization.

PROCESS FLOW

Purchase Request
    ↓
Manager Approval
    ↓
Purchase Order
    ↓
Goods Receipt
    ↓
Invoice Verification


1. PURCHASE REQUEST

Employees must create a purchase request in the ERP system.

The request must contain:

- Item or service description
- Quantity
- Estimated price
- Required delivery date
- Business justification
- Cost center

The employee who creates the request is responsible for providing
accurate information.


2. MANAGER APPROVAL

The purchase request must be reviewed and approved by the employee's
manager.

Managers must verify the business justification, requested quantity,
and estimated cost.

Requests above $10,000 require approval from both the department
manager and the finance department.


3. PURCHASE ORDER

After approval, the procurement team creates a purchase order.

A purchase order must not be created before the required approvals
are completed.

The purchase order is sent to the supplier.


4. GOODS RECEIPT

When the goods are delivered, the receiving team must record a goods
receipt in the ERP system.

The receiving team must verify the delivered quantity and condition.

If the delivered quantity differs from the purchase order, the
difference must be recorded and reported to procurement.

Damaged goods must be reported to procurement immediately.


5. INVOICE VERIFICATION

The accounts payable team verifies supplier invoices.

The invoice must be matched against the purchase order and goods
receipt.

Payment must not be released if the invoice cannot be matched with
the purchase order and goods receipt.


EXCEPTIONS

Urgent purchases may bypass the normal process only when authorized
by the department manager and finance department.

Emergency purchases must be documented after the purchase is completed.


RISKS

Incorrect purchase requests can result in incorrect orders.

Missing approvals can create unauthorized purchases.

Incorrect goods receipts can result in incorrect supplier payments.

Failure to match invoices can result in payment errors or fraud.
